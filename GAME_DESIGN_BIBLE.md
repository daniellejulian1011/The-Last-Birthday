# THE LAST BIRTHDAY — THE COMPLETE STORY & WORLD BIBLE

**Genre:** Atmospheric first-person survival horror / historical mystery / choice-driven psychological tension

**Era:** October 27, 1983. **Location:** Candlewick's Celebration Palace, a ruined 1970s children's birthday-entertainment venue off a remote county road. The fictional locale is deliberately unspecified so a future episode can ground the county and surrounding town.

**Logline:** On the tenth anniversary of the fire that killed her seven-year-old brother, twenty-four-year-old June Mercer returns to an abandoned birthday palace. An anonymous invitation invites her inside, where the original mascot still moves and the evidence of a deadly cover-up is waiting behind chained doors.

**Tagline:** *Some parties never end.*

## Character and emotional arcs

### JUNE MERCER — protagonist (24 in 1983; 14 in 1973)

Quietly brave, curious and hyperobservant. June is not a fearless action hero; she carries a flashlight, a 35mm film camera, a canvas rucksack and a decade of unresolved guilt. Her jacket is an auburn-red windbreaker, worn over a striped top and cuffed jeans, with brown boots. She once promised Robin they would leave together; during the fire they were separated. Her arc: discover what happened without punishing herself for surviving. The first-person player sees her hand, flashlight/camera equipment and her notes, never a third-person protagonist during normal gameplay.

**Character flaw:** She initially confuses finding someone to blame with finding closure. **Character strength:** She insists that her brother was a person, not an entry erased from a guest list. **Theme:** Truth will not reverse loss, but silence prolongs it.

### ROBIN MERCER — June's younger brother (7 in 1973; deceased)

A bright, excitable child whose seventh birthday was October 27, 1973. Robin loved the pink rabbit entertainer, the cake candles and the arcade prizes. He died in the electrical stage fire when emergency routes proved chained shut. He is seen only in archival photographs and the 1973 historical record. The story never treats his death as a fakeout or portrays him as secretly surviving.

### MR. MORROW — the abandoned celebration mascot

Tall pink-and-cream mechanical rabbit with a fixed painted smile, large hollow-looking eye sockets, torn felt ears, burgundy bow tie, three large buttons, blue vest remnants, scuffed oversized shoes and exposed internal harness. In the early 1970s his family-venue performance included prerecorded birthday greetings and cheerful music. His damaged 1983 form moves through the abandoned rooms with a disturbing combination of winding motor starts, stops, slow head tilts, jerking elbow joints and violent bursts of speed. **He is utterly mute in 1983.** The source of his present-day movement is left unresolved; the fire and cover-up do not require unethical experiments or hidden child-voice tests.

**Behavior:** Watches from the stage, follows footsteps, investigates bright light, stalks corridors and sprints on detection. A powerful 35mm flash briefly interrupts him. Audio identity is physical: cable scrape, metal-on-tile footfalls, joint clicks and strained servos; never human speech.

### EDWIN CANDLEWICK — proprietor and architect of the cover-up

Polished and charming in the old publicity material, privately controlling and concerned with expense, order and the hotel's-like prestige of his celebration palace. He ordered chain locks placed across exits under the rationale of maintaining crowd control and protecting staff-only corridors, ignored warnings and later altered security records. He did **not** deliberately ignite the stage: the electrical fire was accidental, but preventable deaths followed directly from his exit policy. His 1983 presence is expressed through an abandoned office, signed orders, files and recovered historical recordings. The story remains focused on his documented decisions rather than cartoon villain monologues.

### RUTH HALE — safety supervisor / witness

Repeatedly reported the chained exits before October 1973. Her dated memos and preserved warnings become vital proof that danger was understood and preventable. She appears only in dated archival information and possible flashback material, never physically in the 1983 palace.

## True chronology

**1970–1972:** Candlewick's becomes a popular party venue. Mr. Morrow performs cheerful family shows. Staff raise crowd-control and fire-egress concerns.

**1973 / Weeks before October 27:** Ruth Hale documents violations. Edwin keeps the east and south emergency doors secured with chains and padlocks while guests are inside.

**October 27, 1973 / 7:14 PM:** A birthday photograph captures June, Robin and Mr. Morrow in the decorated hall. June is 14; Robin turns 7.

**7:42 PM:** The exit-control log notes chained doors.

**7:46 PM:** An electrical failure ignites theatrical materials behind the performance stage. Fire spreads to the stage curtains and adjacent decorations.

**7:52 PM:** Crowds seek a route through the south corridor as emergency egress becomes impossible.

**8:03 PM:** The rescue response is notified. In the ensuing catastrophe Robin dies; June escapes, but they are separated. Other casualties are suggested without assigning fabricated official totals.

**Following weeks:** Candlewick's closes permanently. Public paperwork minimizes the exit-chain policy, and surviving documents are sealed, hidden, removed or misfiled.

**October 27, 1983:** June receives an anonymous invitation and returns with her camera. The dark palace contains clues and a mobile silent mascot. The identity of the invitation's sender remains open-ended.

## Horror language / jumpscares

**The stage awakening:** Once June acquires Robin's photo, the old stage goes dark, a metal shoe drags behind the curtains, and the mascot begins moving. In the playable build, audio and enemy activation are wired; a bespoke cinematic is a later production task.

**False safety:** The arcade fuse restores helpful light and permits the security door to open, but the humming circuit makes June easier to locate.

**The hallway encounter:** The first close pursuit triggers a sharp sting and frightening image flash, followed by visible movement — it is possible to escape, not a scripted unavoidable death.

**The camera stun:** A photographic flash can freeze the mascot in place. It is a deliberate countermeasure with finite film, not a combat attack.

**The catch:** A threatening face rush, static crackle, a dark red wash, then an ending card. No speech, no unexplained voice imitation.

**The final cake:** Low musical notes from the old equipment accompany the final decision. The music may come from an archive machine; no present-day vocal performance occurs.

## Sound direction

Interior low-voltage hum (50–60 Hz), distant transformer resonance, rain beyond boarded windows, water dripping into metal, cable tension creaks, overlapping stiff-footed mechanical footsteps, old cabinet buzz, shutter snap, pounding heartbeat only while being hunted and detuned music-box tones linked to archival childhood imagery. **Never assign any 1983 vocalizations to Mr. Morrow.**

The shipped prototype generates effects through Web Audio. A release-quality version would use multi-layered Foley, dynamic occlusion, impulse-response reverb and adaptive musical stems.

## Endings and ethical tradeoffs

1. **THE TRUTH SURVIVES:** Expose the original evidence and Edwin's signed order; June escapes. Evidence-based best reconciliation.
2. **THE DOORS OPEN:** Restore the water route and force the emergency exits open. The party ends.
3. **ASHES TO ASHES:** Destroy the building, losing material proof along with the threat. Revenge vs accountability.
4. **ALONE IN THE DARK:** Escape without resolving what happened. Survival is not the same as closure.
5. **THE LAST GUEST:** Stay in the room. An ambiguous tragic choice that lets the venue trap June.
6. **THE NEW HOST:** Assume the mascot's place in the party. A deeply unsettling cycle-of-trauma ending.

**Failure scene:** THE PARTY FOUND YOU — Mr. Morrow captures June during pursuit. This is an encounter outcome, separate from the six story endings.

## Future narrative chapters

An expanded version could use interactive 1973 flashback sequences, multiple NPC testimonies in cassette archives, real multi-step breaker routing, hiding closets with breathing control, film-camera evidence development, an alternative stairwell route, and a deeper ethical confrontation with Edwin's business legacy. All additional beats must preserve the 1973 electrical fire, Robin's death, Ruth's archival-only 1983 role and mute-present-day Mr. Morrow.
